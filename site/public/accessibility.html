Accessibility

Last updated: 14 November 2026

“Qibla & Prayer” should work for everyone. This page says what the app does for accessibility today, and how to tell me when something doesn’t work for you.

What the app supports

VoiceOver: every button, icon and control has a spoken label, and the Qibla screen tells you which way to turn. The Qur’an is read with an Arabic voice, and each verse on a page is an action (in the rotor) that opens it to copy, share or play.

Voice Control: every control has a name you can say, matching what you see. Say “Show actions” on a Qur’an page to open one of its verses.

Larger Text: text grows with the text size set on your iPhone, up to the largest accessibility sizes. At those sizes the monthly timetable lists each day’s times instead of seven columns. The Qur’an page also has its own text size in its menu. The tasbih’s count grows until it fills its circle.

Dark interface: the app is dark, with light text, and so is the Qur’an. For a light paper page, choose Paper in its settings.

Contrast: all text has a contrast of at least 6.3 to 1 against its background. The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) ask for 4.5 to 1.

Not by colour alone: the next prayer and the next day in the Islamic calendar have a diamond in front, today’s row in the tables is bold, and a selected choice has a check mark. A bell that is off is crossed out. In the Qur’an, the verse being recited or found is highlighted, and also underlined when Differentiate Without Colour is on.

Reduce Motion: when it is on in your iPhone’s accessibility settings, the compass, the setup and the tasbih counter no longer animate, and the Qur'an's pages slide instead of curling.

Captions: when the Qur’an is recited, the page shows the Arabic text being recited and marks the verse. The recitation is the text on the page, with nothing added.

Haptics: your iPhone taps when you face the Qibla, with every count of the tasbih, and when a round is complete.

Languages: English, Dutch, German, French, Turkish and Arabic. Arabic reads right to left.

Good to know

Makkah live is YouTube's live stream from the Masjid al-Haram, in YouTube's own player: it has captions when YouTube provides them, and no audio descriptions. The app has no other video.

Every build runs Apple’s automated accessibility audit on each screen, at the normal and the largest text size. Such an audit can’t find everything, so please tell me if something doesn’t work for you.

This website

This website is plain HTML with headings and no scripts, and follows your browser’s text size and zoom.

Tell me what doesn’t work

If something in the app is hard to see, hear, reach or understand, please email support@qibla.dev. It helps to mention your iPhone model, your iOS version and the accessibility settings you use.
