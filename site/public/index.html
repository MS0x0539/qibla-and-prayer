An iPhone app for prayer times, the Qibla and the whole Qur'an. Free, without ads, and nothing about you is collected.

Times as your mosque keeps them

Wajh calculates the prayers the way the mosques in your country do, also when you travel, and follows Diyanet when the app is in Turkish. Each time can move a few minutes to match your mosque exactly, and the night shows its middle and its last third.

Questions

Is it free? Yes, all of it: no ads, no subscription, nothing to buy.

Which iPhones? Any iPhone with iOS 18 or later.

Does it work without internet? The times, the Qibla and the text of the Qur'an all work offline. A recitation needs internet, unless you downloaded it first.

The times differ from my mosque's timetable. Mosques use different calculation methods, mostly for Fajr and Isha. In Settings, choose the method your mosque uses: each one shows today's Fajr and Isha, so you can compare them with your mosque's timetable. Check the Asr setting too, and if a time is still a few minutes off, move it under Adjust times.

The compass points the wrong way. Hold your iPhone flat, away from metal, magnets and other electronics, and move it in a figure eight to calibrate the compass. Allow location access, so the compass can use true north instead of magnetic north.

I don't get notifications. Check that notifications are allowed for the app in your iPhone's Settings, that the bell next to the prayer is on, and that no Focus mode is holding them back. Open the app now and then: iOS keeps a limited number of reminders waiting. The app schedules the next days each time you open it, and now and then in the background if Background App Refresh is on for it.
