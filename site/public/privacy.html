Privacy policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

Wajh (called “Qibla & Prayer” on the Home Screen) is made by Melih Sivrikaya. This policy explains what happens to your data when you use the app.

In short: the app sends nothing to me, and I don't collect data about you. There is no account, no advertising, no analytics code and no tracking. To name your place, search for a city and show the map and mosques nearby, your iPhone asks Apple Maps. To play a recitation of the Qur'an, it downloads the audio from Islamic Network. See below.

Who is responsible

I, Melih Sivrikaya (ms@psbv.org), am responsible for this app and for the personal data described in this policy (the “controller” under the GDPR). Apple is responsible for its own services that the app uses: Apple Maps, Siri, crash reporting and iCloud Backup. Islamic Network is responsible for its audio service, which the recitations come from.

The app asks Apple Maps and Islamic Network only for what the features you use need. I answer emails and use the reports Apple passes on because of my legitimate interest in supporting and improving the app (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Your location

The app uses your iPhone's location to calculate prayer times and the direction of the Qibla, to suggest the calculation method used in your country, and to let the compass use true north. That calculation happens on your iPhone.

Some features use Apple Maps, so Apple receives what they need: your location, when the app looks up the name, country and time zone of your place (when it starts, and again after you have moved more than about a kilometre); what you type in the city search, as you type it; and, when you open the Qibla map, the area around you or around the city you chose, to show the map and search it for mosques. Tapping a mosque opens it in the Apple Maps app. The app adds nothing that identifies you. Apple handles these requests under its own privacy policy (apple.com/legal/privacy).

The app asks for your location only while you use it (“While Using the App”), so iOS never gives it your location in the background. It saves the last place it found; Siri's answers and the reminders it schedules in the background use that saved place. You can also use the app without location access: choose a city, and turn location access off for the app in your iPhone's Settings.

What stays on your iPhone

The app stores your settings (including your school and calculation method), the last place it found or the city you chose (name and coordinates), the tasbih count, the tracker (the days you marked as fasting, the fasts to make up and the missed prayers), and your bookmarks and reading progress in the Qur'an. They are kept on your iPhone, not on any server, and are not synced through iCloud. Like other app data, they are included in your iPhone's backups (iCloud Backup or a backup on your computer), if you make them. Deleting the app removes them from your iPhone.

Reminders are scheduled on your iPhone itself; no server sends them. The iftar countdown on the Lock Screen also runs on your iPhone.

Recitations of the Qur'an

When you play a recitation in the Qur'an tab, your iPhone downloads the audio of each verse from Islamic Network's audio service (cdn.islamic.network) and keeps it, so that no verse is downloaded twice; iOS may remove these files when it needs space. Like any website, the service receives your IP address and which verse and reciter your iPhone asks for; the app sends nothing else. Islamic Network says it keeps no logs for longer than 29 days (alquran.cloud/terms-and-conditions).

Siri and Shortcuts

When you ask Siri for a prayer time, Apple handles your question as it does for any app. The app works out the answer on your iPhone, without opening, from the settings and the place saved there.

Crash reports and statistics

If you share analytics with app developers (in your iPhone's Settings: Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements), Apple sends me crash reports and statistics about how the app performs and is used, such as the number of sessions. Apple also shows me totals such as the number of downloads per country. Apple collects this itself, under its own privacy policy, and says it does not identify you; the app contains no code for it. I use it only to fix problems and improve the app.

No advertising or tracking

The app contains no advertising, analytics or tracking code. Besides Apple's own frameworks it uses only Adhan, an open-source library that calculates prayer times on your iPhone and sends nothing anywhere. Apart from the requests to Apple Maps and Islamic Network described above, the app shares nothing with anyone. I don't collect personal data from anyone, including children.

If you email me

If you email me, I receive your email address, your name if you give it, and what you write. I use them only to answer you, and I delete the conversation once it is no longer needed, or sooner if you ask. My email provider stores the emails for me.

Your rights

Apart from emails you send me, I keep no personal data about you. For those emails you can ask me for a copy, ask me to correct or delete them, ask me to limit how I use them, or object to how I use them. Everything the app stores is on your iPhone: you can change it in the app, or remove all of it by deleting the app. Data held by Apple is handled by Apple (apple.com/legal/privacy).

If you think I handle your data wrongly, you can complain to a data protection authority, for example the one in the country where you live.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will appear on this page with a new date.
